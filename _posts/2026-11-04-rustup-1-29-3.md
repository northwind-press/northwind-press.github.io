The rustup team released [rustup 1.29.3](https://blog.rust-lang.org/2026/11/03/Rustup-1.29.3/) on 2026-11-03 to fix two regressions{% include cite.html n=1 %}. One caused an "Illegal instruction" error on certain x86_64 CPUs without SSE4.1, and the other a "`lock()` not supported" error on Solaris{% include cite.html n=1 %}.

## Which release broke it

The [rustup changelog](https://github.com/rust-lang/rustup/blob/stable/CHANGELOG.md) calls both bugs regressions over the previous release, which it lists as 1.29.2, published on 2026-10-20. That release only updated certificate-handling dependencies to address CVE-2026-111943{% include cite.html n=2 %}.

## What to do

Our advice, following the rustup team's instructions: stop any programs that may be using rustup, such as your IDE, and run `rustup self update`{% include cite.html n=1 %}. Rustup also updates itself at the end of a normal `rustup update`{% include cite.html n=1 %}.

If self-update fails, the team suggests a manual replacement{% include cite.html n=1 %}. Download the `rustup-init` binary for your platform, make sure no other rustup processes are running, and replace your `rustup` binary with the downloaded file renamed to `rustup`{% include cite.html n=1 %}. That binary is often at `$HOME/.cargo/bin/rustup`, or `$CARGO_HOME/bin/rustup` if you set `$CARGO_HOME`{% include cite.html n=1 %}.

The team also warns that anti-malware scanners may block a new rustup release, especially when installing `rust-docs`, and expects that to resolve within a few weeks as scanners are updated{% include cite.html n=1 %}.
